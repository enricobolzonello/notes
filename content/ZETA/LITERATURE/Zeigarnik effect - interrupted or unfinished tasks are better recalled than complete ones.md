> [!Definition]
> In psychology, the Zeigarnik effect occurs when an activity that has been interrupted may be more readily recalled. It postulates that people remember unfinished or interrupted tasks better than completed tasks.
>  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeigarnik_effect

The effect has two practical interpretations that pull in different directions — see the child notes.

# See also 
- [[Con la pratica i comportamenti si trasferiscono dal Sistema 2 al Sistema 1]] — completed tasks that have become automatic are exactly what the Zeigarnik effect doesn't apply to: once a task is fully internalised into System 1, there is nothing "unfinished" to hold in memory 
- [[On the symbiosis of remembering, forgetting, and learning]] — the Zeigarnik effect is a retrieval strength phenomenon: unfinished tasks have high retrieval strength because they are still "active"; completed tasks lose retrieval strength as they are deactivated
